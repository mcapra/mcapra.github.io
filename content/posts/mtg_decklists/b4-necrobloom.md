# **Introduction**

Welcome to the Necrobloom primer! 

This is a work in progress that started even before the release of MH3 itself, as this deck evolved from a list I previously had of Yuma, proud protector. It continues to evolve and has been a great effort of love to this archetype. I wish to thank everyone that has shown interest in this deck, commented suggestions and helped me make this deck become the best it can be. Special thanks to everyone in the necrobloom discord for their enthusiasm, suggestions and love for this archetype. You’re a pleasure to bounce ideas with and you’ve contributed a large part to this deck. 

This deck fits the player that likes layered combo. A layered combo deck has less of "A + B" combos and aim to instead create a synergistic combo from many different pieces. You'll understand reading the combo overview that the same pieces exist in many combos and that every combo has many different alternatives for each card. This makes a deck that is dynamic to play and rewards skillful navigation of tutors, draw and politics. This also makes the deck indredibly dangerous for your opponents, because with enough resources to begin with you can navigate around removal by going for alternate combos. 

As a bracket 4 deck it aims to win between turns 3-5. It can definitely go long, but this particular version has foregone strength in the lategame to be evasive, adaptive and fast. If you want to adapt this deck to bracket 3 there are some awesome lists on the necrobloom discord I'd advise you look to before this.

## Combos

### Deterministic

The following combos all result in infinite mana, mill and lands ETB and LTB. 
You’ll get the picture of the combos, they mostly are a combination of different pieces which fulfill a condition. The pieces need to return lands, sacrifice them and gain mana. The most elegant version of this requires 3 cards, little set-up and casts no spells. 

#### Aftermath Analyst

Aftermath Analyst in graveyard + Shifting Woodland + Squandered Resources:

Conditions:
1. There are at least four card types among cards in your graveyard.
2. You control at least eight other mana-producing lands, three of which can tap to produce at least {G}.

Combo:
1. Activate Squandered Resources by sacrificing any land except Shifting Woodland, adding {G} if possible, or one mana of any other type otherwise.
2. Repeat step 1 until you control no other mana-producing lands except Shifting Woodland.
3. Activate Shifting Woodland's second ability by paying {2}{G}{G}, causing it to become a copy of Aftermath Analyst until end of turn
4. Activate Shifting Woodland by paying {3}{G} and sacrificing it, returning it and all other land cards from your graveyard to the battlefield tapped.
5. Repeat.

If you control nine or more other mana-producing lands, this combo will net infinite mana of colors those lands can produce. This counts lands that were in your graveyard at the start of the combo. 
As a pay-off to this, any surveil land will put the entire library into the graveyard and winning through one of the lines specified in the "how to win after executing a combo" section.

This is the go-to combo which I do in 90% of the time. The main strength of this combo is it’s difficult to interact with nature, as it’s immune to creature removal and counterspells, which are the most common interaction spells in EDH. To stop it, either instant-speed enchantment removal on Squandered Resources or graveyard hate can stop it. This combo is also incredibly resilient and can start with only Shifting Woodland on the battlefield and Aftermath Analyst and Squandered Resources in the graveyard.

1. Activate Shifting Woodlands targeting Aftermath Analyst
2. Hold priority, activate Shifting Woodland targeting Squandered Resources.
3. Shifting Woodland becomes a copy of Squandered Resources, before the second Shifting Woodland copy ability resolves, sacrifice lands for mana. 
4. Shifting Woodland becomes a copy of Aftermath Analyst, activate its ability to sacrifice itself and bring back all lands. 

The requirement for this is greater, as you need to generate enough mana from sacrificing lands that you can activate Shifting Woodlands twice.

#### The Gitrog Monster
With the necrobloom out, you can replace the draw trigger from the gitrog monster with a dredge 2, returning the land and milling 2. If you hit a land, then you get another dredge 2, potentially hitting another one. With land sacrificers this most often can go through the entire deck.

There's also cleanup step combo you can perform with enough conditions.

---

The Gitrog Monster + The Necrobloom + Emergence Zone/Out of the Tombs

Conditions: 
1. 8 cards in hand, 1 of which needs to be a land
2. {6}{W} Available (if Emergence Zone)

Combo: 
1. On the Cleanup step, discard the land in hand.
2. The Gitrog Monster trigger to draw a card, replace that with dredge 2, returning the same land to hand and milling 2.
3. On any other milled lands you'll get additional draws, with these you can dredge additional lands to hand.
4. Repeat 2-3 until you've milled the entire deck and now have atleast 8 lands in hand. 
5. Discard all cards in hand except 7 lands (doesn't matter which).
6. The Gitrog Monster trigger to draw a card with an empty library. 
7. If you're going through Emergence Zone, sacrifice it and play Sevinne's Reclamation from your graveyard in response to the draw, returning Out of the Tombs. (Note: You have to sacrifice the Emergence Zone with atleast 1 card in your library or you'll die to the draw.)
8. Replace the draw with Out of the Tombs effect to reanimate Cultivator Colossus.
9. Put the 7 lands in your hand into play and replace each draw trigger from Cultivator Colossus with an additional reanimate. 
10. Return to play atleast Horizon Explorer, Lumra, Bellow of the Woods, Aftermath Analyst, Angel of Indemnity.
11. Angel of Indemnity returns Squandered Resources.
12. If you didn't activate it before, you now have an untapped Emergence Zone available to sacrifice. 
13. Win through any loop you deem fit.

### Nondeterministic
This deck has, in addition to the deterministic combos, nondeterministic combos that can go into the deterministic combos. These consists of 2 main synergies with the necrobloom, that of The Gitrog Monster and Cultivator Colossus. 
#### Cultivator Colossus

This requires 1 land in hand, as many lands as possible in the graveyard and The Necrobloom on the battlefield. This might sound a bit difficult at first but getting a land to hand is incredibly easy thanks to dredging, and putting lands in the graveyard is the entire point of the deck. 

What this enables is one of the most powerful thing this deck can do. What will happen when Cultivator Colossus triggers is that we’ll put a land into play from hand, then we get to draw a card and repeat this process. What is very important is that we can replace the instance of draw with a dredge 2, meaning that now 2 new cards are put into the graveyard. This completely changes the quite poor math of colossus into a something that pretty much always mills the entire deck and puts all lands into play. Quite good for a 1 card combo. 

Keep in mind that the lands enter tapped and no triggers happen until the colossus has fully resolved. Meaning: if you don’t have enough mana to win after fully milling the deck, stop when there’s a couple of cards left so that you don’t accidentally die. 

Dread Return is pretty key afterwards to return something that enables entering a deterministic combo, most often Angel of Indemnity to get back Squandered Resources.

### How to win after deterministic combo without a ping land

This deck uses Sunscorched Desert and an infinite land loop to win the game. Some decks go for the 'crime' land cycle e.g. Festering Gulch for better colors. If you decide to not use this plan, there are 3 other ways of ending the game without a ping land presented here. Note that some of these win conditions name cards currently not in this deck.

---
- Land in hand, which can be guaranteed with Golgari Rot Farm and a land loop.
- Infinite mana 
- Infinite self-mill
1. Get Six to the battlefield. Sevinne’s Reclamation, Angel of Indemnity, Dread Return, Lazotep Quarry and/or Shifting Woodland can all accomplish this task. 
2. Retrace Forgotten Cellar from the graveyard
3. Cast Nuclear Fallout or Finale of Devastation for {X}  = symbol:infinity-c. 
---
- Infinite mana
- Infinite self-mill
1. Get Tyvar, Jubilant Brawler to the battlefield. Sevinne’s Reclamation and Angel of Indemnity are great for this purpose. 
2. Activate Tyvar’s -2 ability to get Shigeki, Jukai Visionary to the battlefield. 
3. Activate Shigeki, Jukai Visionary, putting it to hand. 
4. Channel Shigeki, Jukai Visionary, putting any number of nonlegendary cards from the graveyard to hand. 
5. Cast Nuclear Fallout or Finale of Devastation for {X} = symbol:infinity-c. 

---

- Infinite mana
- Infinite self-mill
- Shifting Woodland on the battlefield.
1. Activate Shifting Woodland targeting Walk-in Closet // Forgotten Cellar
2. Open the room to Forgotten Cellar. 
3. Cast Nuclear Fallout or Finale of Devastation for {X} = symbol:infinity-c. 

### Combos excluded from this deck:

Maskwood Nexus and Life and Limb consists of 2 4-mana permanents that don't have value outside of the combo and it leads to a draw if we can't sacrifice any of the permanents of the combo. 

Perilous Forays + Any land untapper gets us all of our basic lands, but this version of the deck doesn't have enough basics for the true value of this, and land untappers are generally good only when our lands come in tapped, i.e. bad.  

Fluctuator/Bone Miser + lands that cycle for {2} is also a common combo, which isn't in this deck. Similarly, these combos are too reliant on gamepieces that don't have much value outside of the combo.

## Non-obvious, weird and complex interactions

Field of the Dead + Cultivator Colossus: When putting in lands with Cultivator Colossus, they don't enter at the same time which is important for Field of the Dead. This means that you want to put Field of the Dead unto the battlefield as soon as possible in order for it to make the most amount of Zombies. 

Finale of Devastation only grants the +X/+X buff and haste to creatures on the battlefield as it resolves (including the fetch target), meaning that subsequent tokens we make from fetching something like a Cultivator Colossus won't have haste. Do the math with what you have on board!
