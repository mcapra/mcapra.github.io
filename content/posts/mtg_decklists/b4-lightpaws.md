# Primer

**This foxy lady! She even has a Discord server now:** https://discord.gg/rxT2j3PYQT

In my view, the flavor is that each aura attached to her is one of her tails. 

As Light-Paws grows wiser and more experienced, she approaches the full godlike power of a nine-tailed kitsune.

(You have no idea how tempted I was to name this deck "Chasing Tail".)

**Light-Paws, Emperor's Voice** {1}{W}, 2/2

*"Whenever an Aura enters the battlefield under your control, if you cast it, you may search your library for an Aura card with mana value less than or equal to that Aura and with a different name than each Aura you control, put that card onto the battlefield attached to Light-Paws, Emperor's Voice, then shuffle."*

There's a lot to unpack in that ability, so let's dig in.

## OVERVIEW (Dipping your paws into the water.)

The critical aspect to grasp is that Light-Paws is basically Richard Garfield, Ph.D. for auras. 

It's easy to miss that "less than ***OR*** equal" clause at first glance. 

But with a closer look, we discover that every 1-mana aura in the deck is secretly an Ethereal Armor, or a Hyena Umbra, or a Flickering Ward, or... 

Every 2-mana aura is actually All that Glitters or Daybreak Coronet. Every 3-mana aura gives us immortality via Pariah. Every 4-mana (or more) aura is secretly a Sage's Reverie to draw a new hand. 

And so on! 

Even more important, since we run a huge suite of cheap auras with flash, we can tutor for protection spells such as Flickering Ward or Hyena Umbra at ***instant speed,*** to "parry" removal spells as required. 

Put another way, Swift Reconfiguration and Temporal Isolation and Guardian's Magemark (and friends) are all secretly powerful protection spells. 

...Or we can just use them to stick All That Glitters onto Light-Paws at instant speed, for a NASTY (and permanent) combat trick!

Moreover, our "removal" spells (Heliod's Punishment, Darksteel Mutation, Faith's Fetters, etc) can kneecap opposing commanders, while also coming with free card advantage or massive power boosts stapled on. 

With a particularly removal-heavy hand, Light-Paws plays like a tempo deck: it's a *brutal* experience in 1v1 games. Every spell we cast guarantees a 2-for-1. 

And even if our opponents CAN counter our auras, is it worth it? Sure, if you counter our aura, we don't get to tutor. That's fine. We *always* have more auras. Are you SURE you want to burn your $100 Force of Will on my $0.01 Solid Footing?

***TLDR:*** Light-Paws grants mono-white exactly the boost it's always needed.
1) **RAMP:** Light-Paws doubles any mana we spend on casting auras: two mana spent casting an aura results in *four* mana worth of results. Plus, she generates ridiculous mana with Serra's Sanctum or Nykthos, Shrine to Nyx.
2) **CARD ADVANTAGE:** Light-Paws is also a powerful card advantage engine, sculpting every hand we draw into whatever hand our heart desires. Not to mention that we run eight "copies" of Sage's Reverie to refill our hand!
3) **RESILIENCE:** This fantastic fox protects and pumps herself as needed, neatly side-stepping the classic vulnerability of aura-based lists. If we sequence carefully, we can avoid ever leaving a window for removal.
4) **INTERACTION:** Not only do we run eleven instant-speed auras to protect Light-Paws, our aura-based removal always results in us 2-for-1'ing our opponents. Even our removal spells advance our main gameplan!

In general, the auras which show up in nearly every game are Ethereal Armor, All That Glitters, Flickering Ward, Spirit Mantle, and Sage's Reverie. Pariah tends to show up a lot too, in slower and grindier games (see below). 

***LONG STORY SHORT...***

I built Light-Paws thinking she would be silly and janky and linear: "Haha, let's beat people up with a big fox!" 

But having playtested a few times, the synergy is utterly ridiculous. 

I *frequently* kill my first opponents on turn 2 or 3, while still holding up instant-speed protection auras in hand. And unlike other tutor-based commanders, our "toolbox" of options is so broad that I've never gotten bored of playing this list!

## SYNERGIES AND COMBOS (Playing with fox-like grace.)

Light-Paws is a tricky commander to pilot, and the heart of playing her *well* is understanding what to tutor for and why. It's a very skill-testing deck -- we run 42 auras, which (depending on mana cost) can each tutor into between 8 and 36 *other* auras. 

There's no substitute for experience and playtesting. In the Light-Paws discord, each member has a slightly different opinion on what to tutor for and when. 

And our choices shift depending on our opponents and the board state -- whether to prioritize protection or power, evasion or keywords, fast fox-beats or slow-and-steady inevitability. 

And this list is still being tuned and tweaked, so some combos may go in and out over time! But as some general guidelines to constrain the possibility space:

**Tutoring specific "pairs" of auras:**

1) We can cast Mask of Law and Grace tutoring for Shield of Duty and Reason, or vice versa. This is one of our best turn 1 plays, to give protection from all non-white colors for a single mana.
2) Pariah fetches Unquestioned Authority or Spirit Mantle or Timely Ward, making us immortal to damage for as long as we control our commander. Conversely, Timely Ward can fetch Pariah at instant speed to do this trick without our opponents expecting it.
3) Sage's Reverie is the absolute MVP of this deck. We're intentionally heavy on 4-cmc auras just to tutor this beast of a card more reliably. It refills our hand, boosts our commander, and even draws cards for every opposing creature we've locked down with effects like Darksteel Mutation. Note that you can stack Light-Paws's ability to tutor an extra enchantment before resolving the draw trigger. We can tutor Flickerform (or Cloudstone Curio) to create a repeatable "{2}{W}{W}: Refill your hand" effect, even as we protect our commander. Eat your heart out, blue decks!
4) Light of Promise or Sunbond tutor into Daybreak Coronet (or Felidar Umbra), doubling our commander's power every turn as we gain life. Basically, this combo turns Light-Paws into a fox-themed Kalonian Hydra! And if we can also attach Battle Mastery in the process, even 7 power on Light-Paws is a one-hit kill since we effectively get "triple strike".
5) All that Glitters often fetches into Ethereal Armor, to double the power boost once our commander is sufficiently protected. Once Light-Paws is getting a boost for each enchantment we control, we can also cast Lashknife for free and tutor Triclopean Sight for extra value, re-untapping her.

**Card advantage and ramp combos:**

Lack of card advantage and ramp are the reason mono-white is considered to be the worst single color in EDH. How does Light-Paws transcend that limitation?

1) Weathered Wayfarer / Expedition Map / Search for Glory are meant to fetch Serra's Sanctum (or Nykthos) for a HUGE boost of mana. Urza's Saga, when not tutoring for ramp, often grabs Expedition Map to be our "fifth" copy of Sanctum. Remember to float a mana in response to the third chapter ability! Of course, it can also grab Sol Ring or Mox Amber.
2) Our self-bouncing auras (Flickering Ward in particular) let us tutor repeatedly with Light-Paws, and establish a powerful draw engine with Sram, Senior Edificer/Kor Spiritdancer. We can also do this with any 1-drop aura using Cloudstone Curio, for an even lower mana investment!
3) On the subject of draw, our many power-boosting auras make it trivial to give Esper Sentinel 5+ power, guaranteeing us extra draw on nearly all turns.
4) Of course, we're running Scroll Rack + Land Tax as a powerful draw engine in its own right -- as if casting Ancestral Recall every turn. But we can also use Scroll Rack to exchange our hand every turn, since we shuffle with Light-Paws! This synergy is so effective in testing that I'm considering Teferi's Puzzle Box as well...
5) As mentioned above, any 4-mana aura fetches Sage's Reverie to refill our hand as needed. Flickerform makes this massive card draw repeatable.
6) If we control Cloudstone Curio, we never run out of auras to cast. Note that, depending on how you stack triggers, you can choose to bounce the aura you cast, the aura you tutored, or both! I'd recommend tutoring for instant-speed auras then bouncing them back to hand: see below!
7) When we need to recover from losing auras, Mantle of the Ancients is a huge boon to this deck, especially since it can tutor Sage's Reverie -- giving us draw even as we rebuild. This also works very well with Cloudstone Curio if the game runs long, so we can always keep recursion in hand! Similary, Replenish and Retether help recover from this situation.

**Life-manipulation package:**

AKA "Hey look, we're a Karlov of the Ghost Council deck now!"

Light of Promise is just a silly card, okay? 

Silly enough that we run its 4-mana cousin Sunbond.

I already mentioned the pairing with Daybreak Coronet above, but wait! There's more.

Ways we can gain life (and thus, make Light-Paws big in tthe process): Archon of Sun’s Grace, Search for Glory, Aetherflux Reservoir, Celestial Mantle, Daybreak Coronet, Faith’s Fetters, Felidar Umbra.

If we’re gaining life fast enough (and trust me, we can), Aetherflux Reservoir can let us kill more than one player per turn. Or allow us to kill through a “creatures can’t attack” lock, which otherwise sucks. Aetherflux also gives us a way to viably win without Light-Paws (see below).

This "gain life, get counters" approach also stacks very well with double strike, effectively granting triple-strike (and even more the following turns). 

Remember, Battle Mastery + Light of Promise + Daybreak Coronet + any 2 power boost is lethal!

**Leveraging auras with flash:**

Light-Paws turns all auras with flash into powerful instant-speed protection in this deck. Moreover, with Sigarda's Aid, *every* enchantment can take this role as well, even our removal and pump spells. 

Because of the way Light-Paws tutors, we have a LOT of flexibility in how we respond to interaction. Your available answers are only limited by the mana value of the flash-auras in your hand. 

Of course, a 3-mana spell like Guardian's Magemark can fetch any response in the 1 or 2 mana category as well.

*(1-Mana Tutor Targets)*
1) In response to non-white removal on Light-Paws, we fetch Mask of Law and Grace / Shield of Duty and Reason / Flickering Ward.
2) In response to Wrath of God, we fetch Hyena Umbra.
3) In response to single-use Innocent Blood / Plaguecrafter effects, we fetch Cartouche of Solidarity to create an expendable token. 
4) In response to Toxic Deluge or Fire Covenant, we fetch Ethereal Armor (assuming we don't already have it attached).
5) In response to creatures like Thrasios, Triton Hero, we can tutor Detainment Spell, then pay 1W to move it off Light-Paws at instant speed.

*(2-Mana Tutor Targets)*
1) In response to white spot removal, we fetch Benevolent Blessing. This is considered an option of last resort, since we will no longer be able to attach more auras to Light-Paws for as long as Benevolent Blessing is attached. We can mitigate this downside by bouncing it back to hand with Cloudstone Curio.
2) In response to mass removal such as Living Death or Cyclonic Rift or Bane of Progress, if we have 2WW available, we fetch Flickerform.
3) To handle a large attacker (or something like Teysa, Orzhov Scion), we can tutor Spirit Mantle. For blocking specifically, Triclopean Sight is a nasty surprise.
4) If we don't have access to the 1-mana options, we can respond to non-white removal by fetching Cho-Manno's Blessing. Of course, this aura has innate flash, so we could cast it from hand to search for something different on top of its own protection.
5) Similar to the category above, we can dodge toughness-based removal by fetching All that Glitters. Or, to a lesser degree, Saving Grace.
6) Speaking of Saving Grace, we can fetch it for a 1-turn reprieve from all damage (if Light-Paws is protected). At 3 mana, Pariah does this permanently.
7) By fetching Felidar Umbra, we can protect a creature other than Light-Paws if we have an additional 1W available.

*(3-Mana Tutor Targets)*

1) In response to repeatable destroy effects like Attrition, we can tutor Timely Ward for a stronger form of protection than umbras grant. 
2) In response to lethal (or large) damage, we can tutor Pariah. Assuming Light-Paws has some form of protection or indestructibility, we are immortal to damage as long as she stays in play.
3) With a problematic creature on stack, we can tutor Prison Term... then the innate triggered ability shifts Prison Term off Light-Paws and onto the new creature as soon as it enters. This tends to make opponents groan.

**Other useful combos to keep in mind:**

*In no particular order, some other neat synergies I've noticed:*

* Lashknife + Cloudstone Curio + Hallowed Haunting / Archon of Sun's Grace = fetch every aura 2 mana or less to battlefield. It's also a kill with Aetherflux Reservoir.
* Mantle of Leadership can make Light-Paws VERY big, if you control Archon of Sun's Grace or Hallowed Haunting. Some builds use Monastery Mentor in this slot as well, to build up a reserve of tokens to race Dictate of Erebos effects.
* In the worst case, we can use Flickerform to phase out annoying creatures on our opponents' field.

Sometimes our commander is locked out of the game, likely from something like Dictate of Erebos, Solitary Confinement, Opposition Agent, or Swords to Plowshares.  How do we handle this?

*Ways to win when our commander is unavailable:*
* Flickering Ward / Cloudstone Curio + Hallowed Haunting / Archon of Sun's Grace gives us a viable non-commander aggro plan, aided by Serra's Sanctum or Nykthos, Shrine to Nyx.
* Aetherflux Reservoir + Flickering Ward / Cloudstone Curio is a valid way to gain enough life to one-shot people. This can also get silly if we have access to Lashknife as a repeatable free spell, paired with Triclopean Sight.
* If the issue is an enchantment like Solitary Confinement, we can tutor into Mortal Obstinacy and destroy it. Also works to kill Rhystic Study!

**Combos in the maybe-board:**

(These combos are currently removed, but they were interesting enough that they deserve a mention if they're more to your tastes than mine.)

1) If All that Glitters is on our commander, Smothering Tithe makes her absolutely huge. Not that getting 21+ power is HARD here, but still... 
2) Normally Light-Paws only tutors "positive" auras (boosts and protection), since she's forced to attach them to herself. (With the exception of Prison Term, of course, which can move itself to new creatures.) However, with Arden, Intrepid Archaeologist or (flipped) Kitsune Mystic, we can tutor "removal" enchantments onto Light-Paws, then shift them to opponents' creatures as needed!
3) Note that Light-Tail's tutor-and-attach ability doesn't target, and thus *bypasses shroud*. This means we can give her shroud with Lightning Greaves or Greater Auramancy, and still boost her power and protection without ever opening her up for spot removal by targeting other creatures with our auras.
4) If you're running Walking Ballista, there's a whole set of lines worth noting. Even without Heliod, Sun-Crowned, we have several viable options for combos here. We can tutor Light of Promise + Daybreak Coronet onto Light-Paws, then move them over to Ballista with Ardenn, Intrepid Archaeologist. 
5) Moreover, with enough mana, Walking Ballista + Cloudstone Curio + any 3-mana aura is a kill:

*Start by controlling Ballista with 1 counter.*
1) *Cast your 3-mana aura.*
2) *Fetch Light of Promise onto Light-Paws.*
3) *Bounce Light of Promise to hand with Curio.*
4) *Cast Light of Promise onto Ballista.*
5) *Tutor Daybreak Coronet onto Light-Paws.*
6) *Stack Cloudstone triggers to bounce Daybreak to hand.*
7) *Cast Daybreak onto Ballista.*
8) *Win!* 
*Note that Ballista survives with zero counters, since Daybreak is pumping it.*

Total investment: {3}+{2}+{2}{W}+{2}{W}+{W}{W} usually broken into ({3}+{2}{W}) + ({2}+{2}{W}+{W}{W}) over two turns.

With Ardenn, it’s reduced to  {3}+{2}+{2}{W}+{W}{W}, and even less if you have Lashknife available.

Of course, if the auras on Light-Paws get blown up, you could simply drop Walking Ballista, cast Replenish / Retether / Mantle of the Ancients and win. No need for all this complexity!

(This deck is still in flux as I test further, so please excuse if the primer is not 100% up to date.)

## MULLIGAN CHOICES (Hint: It doesn't matter. At all.)

Mulligans are hilariously easy in this deck, since all our cards essentially have built-in "transmute to battlefield".

Ideally we want at least one aura with flash, and at least three cheap auras total. 

Recursion spells like Retether are a plus, but not critical. 

Usually, you want at least one ramp spell: any of the good artifacts, Serra's Sanctum, etc. The goal is to play Light-Paws on turn 1, or on turn 2 while also holding up at least one mana for an aura with flash. That way we're guaranteed protection at all times, so we don't get blown out. 

Here's a real-life example of a "decent" hand, taken from an online game I played recently:

<ul><li>Turn 1: Plains, Sol Ring</li>

  <li>Turn 2: Plains, Light-Paws, pass and hold up {W}.</li> 
  <li>An opponent smugly cast Abrupt Decay on my commander. I responded by casting Solid Footing tutoring for Mask of Law and Grace to fizzle their removal.</li>

<li>Turn 3: Nykthos, Shrine to Nyx. Tap a Plains to cast Curse of Silence, tutoring into Ethereal Armor. Tap Sol Ring to activate Nykthos, add {W}{W}{W}{W}{W}. Cast Unquestioned Authority, tutor for Battle Mastery (and draw a card). I drew Flickerform, which I cast looking for Daybreak Coronet. Light-Paws is now a 14/14 with double strike, vigilance, lifelink, protection from black and red, and protection from creatures. Swing to kill my first opponent.</li></ul>

And that was off of a mull to **five!** 
  
My opening hand was Plains, Sol Ring, Nykthos, Solid Footing, Curse of Silence. My first three draws were Plains, Unquestioned Authority, and Flawless Maneuver (which I never actually needed to use). 

Nothing super ridiculous in that opener, at first glance. 

But since every two-mana aura is basically interchangeable... 

*Every* hand with terrible common auras can prove to be ridiculous.
  
Meanwhile, an absolutely PERFECT hand looks more like: 

<ul><li>Turn 1: Plains, Chrome Mox, Jeweled Lotus, Light-Paws, Shield of Duty and Reason (fetching Mask of Law and Grace), Hyena Umbra (fetching Ethereal Armor). Or vice versa, of course. Now you have protection from UBRG *and* an umbra on a commander with 7 power, on turn 1.</li>

<li>Turn 2: Serra's Sanctum. Tap Plains and Chrome Mox for any two-mana aura, fetching All that Glitters. Tap Sanctum for 6 white, play Angelic Destiny fetching Sage's Reverie. Draw 8 cards. Spend your last 2 mana on Daybreak Coronet to fetch Benevolent Blessing. Light-Paws now is (at least) a 40/40 with flying, protection from all colors, first strike, lifelink, vigilance, and an umbra. Swing and kill an opponent of your choice.</li></ul>

But even without this sort of ridiculous over-the-top god-hand, it's pretty trivial to get a T1 Light-Paws, and stack Ethereal Armor plus All that Glitters plus Battle Mastery on her Turn 2.

A classic example, from another recent real-life game:

<ul><li>T1: Plains, Mana Crypt, Light-Paws, using Lashknife or Flawless Maneuver to grant T1 protection if needed.</li>
<li>T2: Plains, Mask of Law and Grace fetching Ethereal Armor (4 power), Battle Mastery fetching All that Glitters (11 power plus double strike). Swing for lethal!</li></ul>

What are you waiting for? Get to smacking people with a big fox! 🦊

## HOW TO TUTOR IN REAL GAMES (The trickier part.)

A primer can only ever help so much. 

***General / Broad Advice***

My tutoring choices depend on the other players, to some degree. If I see them holding up mana, I'll usually prioritize tutoring protection from their colors first. If they're playing black, I'll focus on getting Cartouche of Solidarity so that a single sacrifice effect doesn't blow me out. If they're playing white, I'll usually kill them first, since white is the hardest color to protect against in this deck. 

Once I have initial protection, I usually prioritize tutoring for Ethereal Armor, All that Glitters, and Spirit Mantle in the super early game (though I try to hold up mana for an instant speed aura). If the game goes on for a little longer, Sage's Reverie is the best way to refill my hand. 

And of course, Battle Mastery and Light of Promise make Light-Paws enter lethal range VERY fast.

Be especially careful around ***white*** players holding removal. 

We dodge their mass removal without too much trouble, with umbra effects or indestructible, but white *spot removal* is the bane of our existence. 

While we *can* tutor for Flickering Ward on white, it will strip all of our other auras off Light-Paws. Benevolent Blessing is a little better, as mentioned above, but it still prevents future auras from working correctly. Spectra Ward doesn't remove auras, but it's a lot of mana and prevents us from targeting Light-Paws with new auras. 

(Whether or not her innate ability works while Spectra Ward is attached is... an open rules question. Stay tuned!)

***More In-Depth Analysis***

In my head, I divide fetchable auras into two categories: reactive and proactive. 

Reactive auras grant protection, indestructibility, or totem armor.

Proactive auras grant power boosts, aggressive keywords, or evasion. 

Auras like Mask of Law and Grace, the ones that grant protection from X color, can fall into both categories. It's the central reason they're so good.

With this in mind:

**The best proactive/offensive auras at each mana value are:**
* 1 CMC: Ethereal Armor. 
* 2 CMC: All that Glitters. Daybreak Coronet. Spirit Mantle.
* 3 CMC: Battle Mastery. Light of Promise. 
* 4 CMC: Sage’s Reverie. Angelic Destiny.

**The best reactive/protective auras are much harder to identify, because it’s very situation-dependent.**

**But a rough overview of our best reactive options:**
* 1 CMC: Flickering Ward. Mask of Law and Grace. Shield of Duty and Reason. Hyena Umbra. Cartouche of Solidarity.
* 2 CMC: Benevolent Blessing. Cho-Manno’s Blessing. Flickerform.
* 3 CMC: Timely Ward. Pariah.
* 4 CMC: None.

You may notice that our reactive auras are skewed towards 1 or 2 mana, while our proactive auras are skewed towards 2 to 4 mana. 

This is intentional -- low mana auras can be more easily tutored from the deck, but high mana auras are more powerful tutors (since they can find a broader variety of things from deck). As in all things, balance is paramount.

The goal in deckbuilding Light-Paws is balancing mana values, to make protection maximally tutorable, while also making the hard-hitting "wincon" auras flexible in what other "paired" auras they tutor. 

***TLDR:*** In a normal game, you want to tutor a solid balance of proactive and reactive options, shifting one way or the other depending on how opponents try to react to your boardstate. The specific auras you choose *within* those categories depend on the many "external" factors given in the sections above.

Beyond this general division, there are two other categories worth mentioning:

**Removal auras -- we accept that they're slow, in exchange for it always being a 2-for-1:**
* 1 CMC: On Thin Ice. Swift Reconfiguration. Curse of Silence. Detainment Spell.
* 2 CMC: Darksteel Mutation. Heliod’s Punishment. Reprobation. Temporal Isolation. 
* 3 CMC: Minimus Confinement. Prison Term.
* 4 CMC: Faith’s Fetters. Faith Unbroken. Curse of Exhaustion.

(We have a great fondness for auras that strip abilities from commanders. Every day, we cry that Kenrith's Transformation and Song of the Dryads are not white.)

**Flash auras -- can fetch into reactive or proactive options as needed:**
* 1 CMC: Gift of Granite. Solid Footing. Swift Reconfiguration. 
* 2 CMC: Benevolent Blessing. Cho-Manno’s Blessing. Mantle of Leadership. Saving Grace. Temporal Isolation. Triclopean Sight. 
* 3 CMC: Timely Ward. Guardian Magemark.
* 4 CMC: None.

(Two-mana flash auras are wonderful because they exist in the liminal space between tutoring proactive and reactive auras, as explained above, and thus can fulfill both roles. They're the stem cells of auras.)

## Powering Up or Down (Tuning to your opponents.)

Light-Paws is strong. 

This is just a fact. She's strong enough to crush most playgroups below high power, even with budget builds. 

Light-Paws can also be somewhat linear. 

This also isn't surprising. After all, consistency is one of Light-Paws's strengths, and you're intending to win through voltron nearly every game. Consistency and linearity are two sides of the same coin. 

I haven't gotten bored of playing her, even after several hundred games. It's a puzzle, defending her and figuring out how and when to kill players. But it's a little solitaire-esque. 

And sometimes your playgroup isn't a fan of repeatedly getting slapped by a giant fox. Or sometimes your playgroup wants to play *higher* power, and they pull out full cEDH decks. 

So! What do we do?

If you want to power *down*, there's two primary ways to do that. I'd remove the fast mana for more cards like Transcendent Envoy or Hero of Iroas, which grant ramp but less ridiculous "turn one Light-Paws with protection" hands. If this isn't sufficient to lower the power level, you can *choose* to restrict your tutoring in various ways. I've tested with treating Light-Paws's ability more like cascade: you flip cards from the top of your library until you find X cards which her ability COULD tutor, then pick one. You could either choose X as a constant at the beginning of the game, or base it on the mana value of the aura you cast from hand. 

I've been testing with X=3 at mid-power casual tables, and the deck seems consistent enough to still be a threat, while not being quite as overpowering.

If you plan to play Light-Paws as "cascade" full-time, I'd remove some of the flash auras (since their toolboxing options are reduced) and swap to a higher number of 4-drops to hit Sage's Reverie more consistently. Flickering Ward is also the MVP in this style of play. 

This is closer to the [$150 budget build](https://www.moxfield.com/decks/a22p-WKzr0WpGWM_4t8dHA) I've made for Light-Paws. 

If you need to power Light-Paws *up*, I've built two distinct cEDH builds for her:
1) [StaxFox 64](https://www.moxfield.com/decks/oMX-AtE6ykayd98MqtpOzw), a 60% voltron / 40% stax cEDH build. It's similar to this list, with enough stax to survive cEDH tables. 
2) [Fantastic Mrs. Fox](https://www.moxfield.com/decks/xAXwMVcb30qADsweho-87A), a collaborative cEDH build made by the Light-Paws Discord server. Higher stax than the other.

Those lists explain things better than I can here. But to swap to StaxFox 64 (my personal preference), this list requires [the following swaps](https://www.moxfield.com/decks/oMX-AtE6ykayd98MqtpOzw/compare/b7JEv8j9d06-BicRKi2Lwg) (at the moment):

REMOVE: Aetherflux Reservoir, Cartouche of Solidarity, Conclave's Blessing, Flawless Maneuver, Guardian's Magemark, Hallowed Haunting, Holy Mantle, Hyena Umbra, Land Tax, Mantle of Leadership, Mantle of the Ancients, Minimus Containment, On Serra's Wings, Prison Term, Replenish, Reprobation, Retether, Scroll Rack, Teferi's Protection, and Temporal Isolation

ADD: Angel's Grace, Archon of Emeria, Aven Mindcensor, Blacksmith's Skill, Cursed Totem, Deafening Silence, Detainment Spell, Devoted Caretaker, Drannith Magistrate, Eidolon of Rhetoric, Ethersworn Canonist, March of Otherworldly Light, Null Rod, Rebuff the Wicked, Rule of Law, Solitude, Static Orb, Stony Silence, Suppression Field, and Torpor Orb

These changes remove some of the more "questionable" or "cute" auras for powerful stax pieces -- particularly stax pieces to shut down Dockside Extortionist, as well as Naus/Breach. 

cEDH pods also have a lot less mass removal and destroy (or sacrifice) based removal, so we can lessen our protections against that type of interaction in exchange for instants which can protect our stax pieces. We also raise our level of removal to handle Opposition Agent (our nemesis).

TLDR: Whether you need something higher or lower powered, Light-Paws has a build for your needs!

## CONCLUSION (Embrace the fox-goddess.)
