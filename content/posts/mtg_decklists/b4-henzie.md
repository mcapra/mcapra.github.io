## Basic Gameplay and Strategy
[![Headline-1-Basic-Gameplay.png](https://i.postimg.cc/yxKwcxqY/Headline-1-Basic-Gameplay.png)](https://postimg.cc/r0hnL8Fv)
### The Only Thing Ziatora Desires is "More." - Glittering Stockpile

Henzie plays like a pseudo Sneak Attack in the command zone. Thanks to blitz, Henzie provides both ramp by discounting the creatures, and card advantage when they die. There are several ways to build Henzie:

* The first is with “Army in a Can” token effects like Avenger of Zendikar or Deranged Hermit. Each blitz will give us a cadre of tokens for defense. Combine this with aristocratic effects like Bastion of Remembrance or buffs like Pathbreaker Ibex and you will have something going. This is frequently a more casual build.

* The second is a “book of monsters” build. This build plays 40+ biltzable creatures and plans on blitzing the whole game. This is also a more casual build as the deck tends to play at sorcery speed and has little interaction on opponents turns. This can also sometimes be a Birthing Pod build that leans into pod effects like Vivien on the Hunt and Eldritch Evolution as much as possible. 

* A third, and similar to the "Book of Monsters" build is a build that seeks to companion Umori, the Collector. Due to Umori, this version plays only creatures, and can be a lot of fun, but suffers from the problems of the Book of Monsters build, but amplified as now we are playing no interaction. 

* A fourth is a *burn* variant of Henzie, is version of Henzie seeks to only leverage blitz targets that deal direct damage, and recycle those targets over and over until the table is dead. [I have built a mock list of this version of the deck by popular request](https://www.moxfield.com/decks/kLKOg_CIKUqvhK3GCRGQ4g).

* This is close to my personal list. The plan is to blitz impactful creatures early, then reanimate the best ones. The deck focuses on high-value ETB/attack/death triggers—think Etali, Primal Conqueror and Archon of Cruelty.

* Not quite CEDH, but close. This version minimizes blitz targets and maximizes combo potential, using Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker, Protean Hulk lines, and Persist combos. It’s tuned with tutors, interaction, and explosive wins.
Some would argue this is CEDH—but if that’s your goal, Korvold, Fae-Cursed King might serve you better as a Jund combo commander.

## Blitz Options

### You Can Still See Their Imprints in the Pavement if You Know Where to Look – Mr. Orfeo, the Boulder

Our Blitz targets come in all shapes and sizes, some we want for ramp and value, others for removal, still others are game winning beaters and board wipes. It is literally a candy shop of choices. The reason Henzie is called “Toolbox” Torre is that you can bring the appropriate tools to any meta.

* Goreclaw, Terror of Qal Sisma – Ideally not a blitz target, Goreclaw provides ramp and a board advantage the longer she is around. Still, I have blitzed her in a pinch, as a small overrun effect, and you can too. The downside is that this is ramp that can get removed. Whereas ramp creatures that find lands furnish us with much more stable mana. 

* Primeval Herald – A Henzie All-Star, fetching multiple lands and replacing herself, Herald is great!

* Rampant Rejuvenator – Another All-Star as it is similar to Herald as it fetches multiple lands. 

* Solemn Simulacrum – Solemn shines in Henzie. It fetches a land, and draws us two cards. Furthermore, we don’t have to wait for it to die because blitz will kill it reliably. 

* World Shaper – It gets better the more fetch lands you play. I have had games where I only get one land, but I have also had games where I get six. Again, the more fetches the better.

* Soul of Windgrace – This is only an option if you are heavily into fetch lands, or budget lands like Ash Barrens or Evolving Wilds. 

* Seedguide Ash – an MVP of the deck, not only does it fetch 3 lands, but it will fetch dual typed lands. 

* Blossoming Tortoise - Better with fetches and slightly higher land count decks, say 36ish, Raph can be a 3 for one and that ain't bad.

* Ojer Kaslem, Deepest Growth - This card is dumb, it not only ramps you when it dies (as it flips into a land), but it also, very likely ramps you again by getting another land off it's attack trigger... * Roxanne, Starfall Savant - A fantastic addition to any ramp package as she is both **ramp and removal** in one. There is nothing better than killing your opponents commander while you are ramping your own board. 

* Overlord of the Hauntwoods - Overlord makes 2 lands upon entry and attack making him a premier blitz target for high budget Henzie builds.

While I have only done this a couple of times, I have found the floor of "3 for 1" to be good enough.

### In Defense of Massacre Wurm

[![635032493513828679.jpg](https://i.postimg.cc/wjrHX5RD/635032493513828679.jpg)](https://postimg.cc/w1DSHmVB)

There has been some discussion as of late regarding the effectiveness of Massacre Wurm. I am unabashedly a proponent of the card as it has won me numerous games on its own, and I would like to offer a defense of the card and some reasons why it is so effective and that you might consider playtesting it yourself.

#### The Upside

1. I will start with the obvious, Massacre Wurm decimates token decks. The effect is so potent against these decks that many of them just straight die on entry, or their life total becomes so low that, coupled with the loss of their board, they are effectively no longer in the game. I have always joked that Henzie is called “Toolbox” Torre because he has all the necessary answers in his toolbox. Massacre Wurm is a vital one for token decks. In fact, the effect is so good that many of us play Balefire Dragon and Primaris Eliminator for the same reasons.

2. However, in addition to this, of the top 42 most played (non-Commander) creatures on EDHrec.com, Massacre Wurm kills 33 of them. That is roughly 79%. Meaning that if there were no token decks at the table at all, Massacre Wurm is likely still very relevant and will kill a couple of creatures per player.

3. At first glance, Massacre Wurm seems less good against creatures who are played as commanders. In fact of the top 100, Massacre Wurm only kills 15%. One would hardly call that game breaking. However, if we look more closely, we can see that he is still meaningful tech against many of them. In fact, of the top 12 commanders on EDHrec, 8 of them either make tokens or encourage the play of small creatures: Lathril, Blade of the Elves, Isshin, Two heavens as one, Yuriko, the Tiger’s Shadow, Wilhelt, the Rotcleaver, Edgar Markov, Shorikai, Genisis Engine, Krenko, Mob Boss, and Go-Shintai of Life’s Origins.

4. What if Gray Merchant of Asphodel killed creatures? Here is a thought experiment. Many Henzie lists play Gray Merchant of Asphodel because it drains opponents of their life. Henzie + Gray Merchant means the floor of Gray Merchant is 3 life per opponent. Meaning 9 total life is lost from all players. As long as Massacre Wurm kills 5 total creatures, it is doing more damage than Gray Merchant… albeit without the life gain… except Massacre Wurm is removing creatures as well. 

5. Massacre Wurm has a strong interaction with Living Death effects. If Wurm is in the yard, when Living Death resolves, it helps us break parity with our opponents by killing any value creatures, small typal creatures, etc. that might return for them, and burns them with damage in the process. Even if it is not in the graveyard, Wurm is good on the board when Living Death effects resolve as it's static ability still triggers for each creature leaving play.

6. While we blitz most of our creatures, Massacre Wurm is very strong when reanimated as its effect ("Whenever a creature an opponent controls dies, that player loses 2 life.") is a static one. Meaning as other creatures die during the game, you are still dealing damage even after the ETB has resolved. This can be effective in general, but shines against sacrifice based decks. 

There is also discussion that running one Primaris Eliminator or a similar effect is enough that you don’t need Massacre Wurm, and if you run enough tutors you can find this effect whenever you need it. My list currently runs 6 tutors… If I ran only 1 Primaris Eliminator effect and these tutors I am 80% to see 1 by turn 8. If I run both Wurm, Eliminator, and Baleful Dragon, (each one good in its own right, and serves a similar purpose) my odds become 88%. I think most of you can tell by now that I am a fan of consistency and redundancy. The main upside that Wurm has against these two (and remember I run both in addition to Wurm) is that Wurm effects all opponents unlike Balefire Dragon, and Eliminator, and unlike Dragon is harder to interact with as it can’t be removed before combat. 

#### The Downside

The downside of Wurm is his casting cost. 3{B}{B}{B} is nothing to sneeze at. Means we need 28 sources of black to cast it on turn 6. Even my list doesn’t have 28 black sources (I have 26 land sources + 7 dorks). Yet, I have never had a problem casting Wurm. Which begs the question, why? Afterall the math says I should. However the answer is fairly simple. I fetch with Wurm in mind. Once I have 2 of every color, the next fetch is for a black source. Henzie fetches quite a lot of basics and dual lands from our decks. Fetching with your hardest to cast spell in mind is inherently correct no matter what deck you are playing. The only real reason I can see not to play Wurm is if your mana base will not accommodate it. Other than that, I think it is pure upside.

## Spin the Wheel
[![Headline-3-Spin-the-Wheel.png](https://i.postimg.cc/CMvYyCbF/Headline-3-Spin-the-Wheel.png)](https://postimg.cc/YjmTYWXV)
### Ziatora’s second-in-command has a habit of appearing when and where her enemies least expect her. - Ognis, the Dragon's Lash

Henzie is a deck that can thrive off of wheel spinning effects. Some of the most played ones are:

* Gamekeeper
* Apex Devastator 
* Kethek, Crucible Goliath 
* Ojer Kaslem, Deepest Growth

These creatures allow us to flip cards off the top of deck in hopes of hitting/cheating a large splashy creature into play. I have played them all, and I have cut most of them. Which begs the question why?

The answer, **variance!** 

### Gamekeeper and Kethek, Cruicible Goliath
#### Gamekeeper and Kethek

Let's figure out if you should cut them too. First up Gamekeeper, when it dies, we reveal cards until we hit a creature, then put it into play. Below is a copy of my current build. which runs 40 creatures but only wants to spin into about 28 of them. The other creatures are mana dorks and value pieces that are not worth casting Gamekeeper to cheat. Assume that anything we want to blitz is a successful cheat target. 

[![Game-Keeper-Flip-Chart.png](https://i.postimg.cc/bYD6nKnh/Game-Keeper-Flip-Chart.png)](https://postimg.cc/WtV6LYSW)

In this chart, I have a 70% chance to hit a real cheat target. This may seem pretty good. However, my experience is that hitting a mana dork is terrible. Three mana for anything that is actually cost {3} mana and has no board impact is rough. As a result and for consistency's sake, I have cut this effect. You can use the same chart for Kethek, Crucible Goliath as he is also flipping for the first creature off your deck. However, each legendary in your deck makes his odds **roughly 2% worse** than these numbers. I would need closer to 34 (hits) to feel good about Gamekeeper or Kethek's consistency.

### Birthing Ritual
#### Birthing Ritual

[![Birthing-Ritual.png](https://i.postimg.cc/sfGBNfDz/Birthing-Ritual.png)](https://postimg.cc/Yjkq4HWD)

Birthing Ritual is a really interesting “fixed” Birthing Pod effect. In fact, the card reminds most Henzie players a great deal of Industrial Advancement. This begs the question, which is better, and should we play one over the other? Should we play both?

Let’s address the simplest aspects of the card first:

* Birthing Ritual is only 2 cmc. This aspect of the card sets it apart from Industrial Advancement. In fact, I cut Industrial Advancement in large part because it’s cmc frequently required taking a turn off to play it. However, Birthing Ritual has the potential to be cast a then followed by a blitz creature the same turn. 
* Birthing Ritual allows players to look at the cards before they sacrifice the creature. Many members of the discord are wildly excited about this aspect of the card, but I am more tepid. Yes, this is an upside, but I also think it is very likely that we are sacrificing the creature regardless of whether we look or not. Afterall, most of the time, the creature we want to sacrifice is the one we just blitzed. Therefore, It doesn’t really matter if we look or not, as we were already going to lose the creature in question. There is something to be said for being able to look if we have reanimated a threat. For Example, we reanimate Incinerator of the Guilty but upon triggering Birthing Ritual, we can sacrifice it if we see something better like Etali Primal Conqueror or keep it if we only see a late game Solemn Simulacrum
* Some have argued the card will place less of a target on our backs, and while that may in true in comparison to Birthing Pod, this will be another must remove value piece for opponents and will only fly under the radar for the first game, and maybe not that long.

#### The Math

The question is how often Birthing Ritual will find us a pseudo “Pod” target. The best part of Birthing Ritual is that we don’t have to Pod up in cmc every turn like Birthing Pod. Instead, we can Pod sideways or even down if we are desperate. 
For the purposes of the math, I am going to term podding down into a mana dork, a fail in terms of what constitutes a successful trigger of Birthing Ritual. Yes, a dork is better than nothing. However, at the power level I typically play at podding into a mana dork is akin to driving a Corvette 35 mph. Yes, you can do that, but that’s not why we bought the Corvette.

[![Birthing-Ritual-Spreadsheets.png](https://i.postimg.cc/vZwTZJ7R/Birthing-Ritual-Spreadsheets.png)](https://postimg.cc/qzQ4mZZQ)

So the math is actually really promising. The typical play pattern in our deck is going to be T1 Dork, T2 Henzie, T3 we will not want to cast Birthing Ritual here, as it is akin to taking a turn off, so blitz. T4 This is likely our first opportunity to cast Birthing Ritual and a blitz target in the same turn with either a 4 drop, or a 5 drop if we ramped with our T3 blitz, so the 4/5 drop math is the most relevant. With a fail rate of only about 20%. Not actually terrible, and if we trigger it twice, we are 96% to get some value that will recoup our mana investment. 

Okay, how does Birthing Ritual compare with our old friend Industrial Advancement?

[![Birthing-Ritual-Industrial-Advancment.png](https://i.postimg.cc/fW3gwpTf/Birthing-Ritual-Industrial-Advancment.png)](https://postimg.cc/HjgtzSMr)

Wow, there are nearly identical… That’s actually a vote in Birthing Ritual’s favor as it cost less, and you don’t have to sacrifice until you view the cards. I still think that is not the giant upside everyone thinks it is, but it is better than Advancement. 

Alright, we have the data, let’s draw some conclusions:
* Birthing Ritual is superior to Industrial Advancement, that much is clear. The math is basically the same, with Ritual costing less mana to cast, and having more upside because we get to peak at the cards before we sacrifice.
* Birthing Ritual is basically the same as Advancement, so it is not better than original Birthing Pod, getting “The Right Creature” is always better than getting “a” creature, and Birthing Pod does not have a failure case. 
* It is possible that tutor heavy decks take a pass on Birthing Ritual. Afterall, it does have a fail case, and in high power metas, getting the perfect card for a situation is better than “getting a random creature” and failing roughly 15-20% of the time.

There is no reason that most casual decks should not be playing Birthing Ritual. In fact, I think there is an argument for play Ritual and Industrial Advancement depending on the build an power level.

It is extemely important that we be able to cast our spells on curve. Afterall, the difference between being able to blitz and not being able to blitz is the difference between winning and losing the game. Furthermore, the fun part of Magic is casting our spells. How do we do that? Well, like any good Riveteer it is all in our foundation and base, Mana base. The easiest thing to do is look at our hardest to cast creature in each slot and do the math for how many sources of that color we need. Once we do the math for one creature, we can apply that math to any other creature with a similar casting cost. With the caveat that all this math excludes ramp or fast mana, let's look at a few:

Henzie "Toolbox" Torre
{B}{R}{G}

We need to find a successful deck that depends on curving out early, just like us. How about we use CEDH Yuriko as a guide? While we aren't playing CEDH, we do want to cast our spells on curve just like Yuriko, and can, therefore, use her percentages as a guide. The standard CEDH Yuriko list runs 23 blue sources, and is therefore 95% to play Yuriko, the Tiger's Shadow on turn 2. With their mana rocks, they are actually a little better than that. I think we can safely aim for 90-95% for our deck and perform just fine. Base on our chart, we need at least 18 sources for each color to hit your land drops and play Henzie on curve.

### How Many Fetch Lands Should I Run?

#### How Many Fetch Lands Should I Run?

How many fetches should we run? Well the answer has got to be 9 right? I mean we can play 9, so 9 is the best. However, I think there is a caveat here:

* **YOU NEED ORIGINAL DUAL LANDS**
* Or you need surveil lands/the 1 Triome

Without original dual lands, you will have fail to find moments if you are running 9 fetches, or I should say, you will have moments were your off color fetches can only fetch basic lands, and you can no longer count all your fetches as “tri-colored” lands when doing the math for your mana base. For example, if Stomping Ground is in your opener, and you fetch out Overgrown Tomb drawing the Misty Rainforest in your deck is now a Forest. It is not also another source of black, and at this point your mana is no where near fixed for your difficult to cast spells. 

[![Surveil.png](https://i.postimg.cc/sXMLG3hH/Surveil.png)](https://postimg.cc/tZHkLQDF)

This problem can be mitigated by play surveil lands like Commercial District or the triome Ziatora’s Proving Ground. The problem here is that these will force you into mulligans if you are insistent upon casting T2 Henzie every game. In fact, if you played all 3 surveil lands and the 1 triome, you would have 26% chance to see one or more in your opener every game. I currently play 1 surveil land and I am only 7% to see it in my opener. 

* 1 of the 4 = 7%
* 2 of the 4 = 13%
* 3 of the 4 = 19%
* 4 of the 4 = 26%

If you study decks in Modern and Legacy that play Surveil Lands, they typically only play 1 ETB tapped Surveil land if they are proactive T1 decks. This includes very proactive decks like Domain Zoo (which wants to play a 1 drop every game). This is because they don’t want it in their opener they want to fetch it on a turn they don’t need the mana. Since they only play 1, the odds of it being in their opener is 11%. 

If we take this lesson to heart, I would not play more than 2 fetchable, ETB tap lands if you are min/maxing for T2 Henzie (Play 2 typed as Forest for Seedguide Ash). 

If you have OG Duals + fetch lands, +  shock lands, + 1-2 triome/surveil lands – we have 7-8 targets for our fetch lands. This is nearly 1 to 1 and is excellent. Even at 9 fetch lands. The worst fail case is that we draw a couple and then Seedguide fetches out 3 more. Yes, some of our fetches will be pseudo basic lands, but after Seedguide we are color fixed for the game anyway. 

Also, no matter how I crunch the numbers, the answer is yes, your deck should play 9 fetch lands. For those of us who are playing OG duals, Shocks, etc. There is simply no reason not to. The optimal basic land package is probably 3 Forests, 2 Swamps, and 2 Mountains being as you need 5 basics assuming you see all your basic land blitz targets in one game (or more likely, recycle Primeval Herald twice). 

If you are on 3 shocks + 1 Triome + 1 Surveil land (and pushing T2 Henzie) then I think you can go to 6-7 fetches before encountering the problem of your fetches becoming pseudo basics before you are color fixed. You will want 3 of them to be the on color Jund Fetch Lands and 3-4 off color evenly distributed amongst {B}{R}{G}. 

## The Best Cards You Can't Blitz

[![Headline-6-The-Best-Cards-You-Can-t.png](https://i.postimg.cc/fyq2RfNX/Headline-6-The-Best-Cards-You-Can-t.png)](https://postimg.cc/XBFc2FX7)
### Restless Riveteers can always find a sparring partner in the sprawling Treza warehouse. -Ziatora's Proving Ground

While casting giant beaters is certainly advantageous and a ton of fun. Some of the best cards we can play are not stompy threats, but rather value pieces and mass reanimation spells. However, by the time we add 30+ Biltz targets, 13 ramp spells (more on this later), and a handful of reanimation spells and removal spells... well the deck starts to fill up pretty quickly. This means that each spell we are playing that isn't one of these needs to be exceptional in order to carry it's own weight. Below are a few that I think are worth it.

* Living Death – If you can only play 1 card from this section, this is the card. I have won more games on the back of a well timed Living Death than almost any other card. Our opponents’ boards get wiped and we get back every scary card we have played all game long. In fact now we have this on a stick with Bringer of the Last Gift.

* Incarnation Technique – We are going to have the mana to cast it, we will have the fat creatures to reanimate, and there is always an opponent to pick who will be unable to capitalize on the effect so that we can resolve it twice. Cool! Hey opponent, you can bring back that mana 4 drop whatever, I am getting Etali and Archon. However, the best part of this spell, is that if you only have 1 target in the yard, you can cast it and copy it anyway. Afterall, you will mill 10, the odds of milling a new target is around 97% for this build. Heck, the odds of milling a threat into an empty yard are 84% for each copy of Incarnation Technique... ain't nothing wrong with that, and we are never going to have an empty yard. Incarnation also doesn't require targets when you cast it, so it can't be Deflecting Swatted.

* Skullclamp – Even if you don’t have tokens to clamp, just attaching it to a blitz’d creature is still bananas. 1 mana to attach it, and draw 2 when the blitz'd creature dies. A lot of Players swear by Drivnod, Carnage Dominus and one of the stated reasons is that is doubles your card, but clamp does this for far less mana. 

* Greater Good – One of the biggest fears of any Henzie deck is running out of gas… have no fear when Greater Good is here, you will never run out of gas, and your graveyard will be full in short order. You blitz a large creature, attack, sac it to Greater Good, draw off the blitz trigger, and then draw for Greater Good, sculpting the perfect hand. It's casting cost can be awkward, but if you untap with it, you are likely winning the game. Think of it as a Wheel of Fortune, but you only discard 3 cards and only after you draw your new seven. You can even discard your new beaters to the yard as fuel for a mass reanimation spell. 

* Birthing Pod- He is called “Toolbox” Torre for a reason. Pod will let you find the hammer, the wrench or the Masscre Wurm  whenever you need it. It can be a little mana intensive, but if you untap with it, again, you are very likely winning that game. Side note, you should not play Birthing Pod unless you have the mana and a target to activate it with. Otherwise, it will likely be gone before you untap. 

* Rise of the Dark Realms - It has a heck of casting cost and a pretty decent price tag, but it does bring back everything... and I mean everything. This is another spot where Anger shines... you just have to able to put him back in the yard, because we are bringing everything to the field. I have cut this one because of its mana value; however, it is still a very strong card and worth playing, especially if you are not playing tutors to help find Living Death the more mass reanimates we can play, the better off we will be.

* Birthing Ritual - A new addition from Modern Horizons 3, and  I new it would be good, but it's actually great. Industrial Advancement is a commonly played value piece. The problem is that it costs four mana. This makes it hard to blitz and cast Advancement in the same turn. Birthing Ritual Solves this by being only 2 mana. It is very possible to blitz and cast this the same turn to get immediate value. It has been a straight up all-star addition to our arsenal. 

## Board Wipes

[![Board-Wipes.png](https://i.postimg.cc/VLB5Xdrp/Board-Wipes.png)](https://postimg.cc/3dNKTrZF)

### “Fallen angels should stay fallen.”—Henzie “Toolbox” Torre -Atraxa’s Fall

Let’s talk a little bit about a question a get a lot, and a card I see often: Blasphemous Act

[![Blasphemous-Act.png](https://i.postimg.cc/Qt4dn8zj/Blasphemous-Act.png)](https://postimg.cc/Q97DF3sz)

The question I get is why I don’t run it, and when I am asked to look over other players decks, it is a card I often suggest that they cut. When you suggest players cut board wipes, they look at you funny. Afterall, conventional wisdom says, you should play board wipes. Conventional wisdom says that Blasphemous Act is one of red’s best board wipes. Ergo, you should play Blasphemous Act. My advice to you:

#### *Doubt the conventional wisdom unless you can verify it with reason and experiment.*

Let’s take a minute and segue to stax and CEDH. Nearly every CEDH plays stax pieces. Let’s look at Yuriko’s stax package.

[![Yuriko-Stax-Package.png](https://i.postimg.cc/QxZDbK4j/Yuriko-Stax-Package.png)](https://postimg.cc/SjdHQRbP)

The deck plays pieces that turn off the graveyard, turn off activated abilities, and turn off rocks. Why these pieces? Well, because Yuriko does not use the graveyard, or activated abilities and the deck plays very few rocks, especially by CEDH standards. 

Just stay with me a moment longer and look at Sisay, Weatherlight Captain’s Stax package:

[![Stax-in-Sisay.png](https://i.postimg.cc/jjB3b9FP/Stax-in-Sisay.png)](https://postimg.cc/yJXyhfB8)

This package focuses on limiting the number of spells played, and reducing interaction on Sisay’s turn. Why? Sisay cast relatively few spells. Instead, it uses Sisay’s activated ability. Instead, it wants to be free to do so on its own turn without hinderance. 

![Learn]( https://i.giphy.com/media/v1.Y2lkPTc5MGI3NjExdzI0ZnI3ZXQ5amNpNmZhNzl1b2tydXIycjZ2NDBieXhiZXV2eW12aiZlcD12MV9pbnRlcm5hbF9naWZfYnlfaWQmY3Q9Zw/RJ0AvNacl2ZSU/giphy.gif)

We need to learn from this. We should play board wipes that don’t interfere with our own plans. Cards like Blasphemous Act and Toxic Deluge are incredibly common in Henzie builds, but actually detract from our own game plan. Instead, we should play board wipes that accentuate our plan. Like these:

[![Board-Wipes.png](https://i.postimg.cc/RCncnhb7/Board-Wipes.png)](https://postimg.cc/pyR9wWvr)

The best board wipes in Henzie are strapped to bodies. These allow us to continue our plan of blitzing, continue removing threats, continue drawing cards, and leave our board completely intact. Furthermore, they are much easier for Henzie to tutor up. 
* Balefire Dragon – A hero, send 1 player back to the stone age. Flying makes it hard to block, and 6 damage removes basically any threat. 
* Incinerator of the Guilty – Does a solid Balefire Dragon impersonation. Don’t let the collect evidence text trip you up. I have never had a problem with it. Remember, you don’t have to exile creatures out of your yard. You can exile any kind of spell. 
* Massacre Wurm – Picks up all the little bodies on board and sometimes just straight wins games. If you have read this primer, you will know I am a fan.
* Primaris Eliminator – Does a solid Massacre Wurm impersonation for a single player, but adds the flexibility of being single target removal as well. 
* Living Death – That’s right, it’s a board wipe. I know; you were saving it for that big turn K.O. Don’t don’t be scared to wipe the board if need be. It’s saved my life on more than 1 occasion.
* Bringer of the Last Gift – Another Living Death, another board wipe. Again, this is plan we are already trying to execute, but with an emergency mode of *board wipe*.
* Culling Ritual – I am not a fan of this in my turn 2 Henzie builds as it eats our board (specifically our mana dorks), but if you are a T3 build using spell based ramp, well Culling Ritual can be very, very explosive and a board wipe to boot. 

One of the strongest things you can do in Magic is breaking parity with your opponents. This is why planewalker decks play lots of creature board wipes. They don’t affect them. It why decks with small creatures play Dusk/Dawn and similar spells, and it’s why you should not play wipes that punish your own board. Just like the CEDH decks, choose cards that don't affect your gameplan, just your opponent's.

## Notable Exclusions

### Everything from damaged furniture to inconvenient corpses becomes fuel for Ziatora’s ever-burning foundry. -Pyre-Sledge Arsonist

**There are cards found in Henzie decks that play experience as taught me are not actually very good, and if you are thinking of running them, or still running them, perhaps reconsider.**

* Ziatora, the Incinerator – Ziatora is the boss of our guild. Don’t tell her, but she kinda sucks. Here is the thing, she is six mana, and she doesn’t want to be blitz’d. There are so many better things that we can be playing in this slot that give us some kind of immediate advantage, and if we can’t blitz it, it had better be damn good, Ziatora is not. If you want a big dragon that makes treasures play Old Gnawbone or Ancient Copper Dragon. I don’t play these either, but I would play them over Ziatora. 
* Cauldron of Souls – You tap it and bring your creatures back, what could be wrong with that? The answer is twofold. One, we kinda want our stuff to die. That’s kinda the whole point of many of our creatures. They bring others back from the yard. Second and more importantly, it is 5 mana, meaning that we are taking off a meaningful turn where we could be casting a blitz target for this and it kinda puts a target on our back. I can see playing this in a casual build that does not feature much other reanimation. 
* Deathreap Ritual – I see this is a lot of lists, cut it! It doesn’t actually work with Henzie as it requires the creature to have died before your end step, meaning our blitz creatures that die “at the beginning of your end step” don’t trigger it. 
* Protection Racket – Joey, on the EDHREC Youtube channel suggested this card for Henzie, and lots people play it. Joey is a smart guy, and he is completely wrong on this card. All this does is guarantee that we don’t draw our best cards as players will pay to bin them, and that the table has knowledge of our hand. Just yuck! If you are really interested I have included his video for reference.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SBLewUqyzNI

* Myth Unbound – We should be ramping anyway. Recasting Henzie is not normally a big deal. You can play something more impactful or use this slot to eat your vegetables and play some kind of interaction. Trust me, you don’t need this. 
* Sundial of the Infinite – It costs so little, it must be good, and in my experience… it isn’t. Paying {1} to use it is basically raising the cost of the creature back up to normal and we don’t get to draw the card on end step because we used the Sundial to end the turn. It just always played awkward for me. The same thing for Temur Sabertooth.
* Lurking Predators – Another common inclusion, and another card recommended by Joey from EDHREC. Lurking Predators can be good, but Joey never bothers to explain why or how. Okay, so it is 6 mana and it only looks 1 card deep, so we take a turn off from blitz to cast it, and let’s say we have 33 creatures cmc 4 and up that we would want to hit. If each opponent casts a spell we are 70% to hit 1 of those creatures. Now ask yourself, if you took a turn off to cast this, and all you hit was Flametongue Kavu are you happy? Now remember that my build is only running 28 creatures I want to hit… You will have games where you hit a lot of bangers and go nuts, but you are also going to have games where you miss a lot. For me, I would want 40 targets (Excluding mana dorks) to make Predators worth it. 
* Korvold, Fae-Cursed King Recommended by the Command Zone Podcast, and appearing in a ton of lists as a result… Honestly,… I don’t know what they were thinking that whole episode… So many “If you add one card add this one” moments… and they are bad and some are expensive, like this one. We don’t want a 5 mana creature we can’t blitz. Korvold only accrues value if we leave him on the board and he basically reads, when your blitz creature dies, draw another card. I know he plays well with fetches and other sacrifices like treasures, but if the creature is 5 or more mana, and we aren’t going to blitz it, it had better be insane. Play Drivnod, Carnage Dominus if you want this effect, he does a whole lot more. I have talked with other players who have argued that the deck gets better because you just add in more sac stuff like fetchs and Dockside Extortionist and other treasure synergies… Here is the thing. Don’t tell Henzie, but Korvold is a more powerful commander, and if you want to lean into his synergies, just put him in the command zone. 

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ogh52RYCUm0

* Selvala, Heart of the Wilds – Stupid Command Zone,….Selvala is a banger, but here everything comes down to her casting cost. She wants to be played the same turn as Henzie… El Torre is an jealous commander. He doesn’t like you looking at other 3 drops. Selvala means Henzie on Turn 4, and our first blitz on turn 5 drawing only 1 extra card. In my meta, the game is half over… Selvala is a good card, she is just too awkward for our curve.
* Morbid Opportunist I bet you can figure out why I don’t like this card either… Hey look it is 3 mana… and it is capped at 1 card per turn… yuck… If you are playing a budget build and want this effect play Braids, Arisen Nightmare or Grim Haruspex.

### Should You Play Deathrite Shaman?

Wow… okay that is strong. However, our decks are so much larger than legacy decks and we are only playing 7 fetch lands. Furthermore, we only have a 41% chance to draw our own fetch lands which is a 59% fail rate. However, we do have 1 advantage, we have more opponents… 

If everyone at a table is running 7 fetchlands:

**Our fail rate is 59% or .59 x Oppon. 1 .59 x Oppon 2 .59 x Oppon 3 .59 = .1211**

**.1211 is a fail rate of 12.11% or a success rate of = 87.89%**

Well, that is better than legacy play… it’s hard not to love that. ### Should I Play Drivnod?

[![Drivnod.png](https://i.postimg.cc/xd9JHCdM/Drivnod.png)](https://postimg.cc/mP6gWTCg)

*Drivnod is a common add in Henzie decks as he doubles dies triggers. However, how good he is varies based on the way your deck is built. Let's get into the math*

#### What is Success?

Determining what a successful use of Drivnod is goes a long way towards determining if he is right for you. Drivnod will double all our death triggers, including those from Henzie. This means that every creature that dies will now draw us 2 cards instead of 1. This alone is enough for some players to include Drivnod, but in my experience it is not enough. Drivnod costs 5 mana, {3}{B}{B}, and also requires us taking off a turn to cast him. Therefore, if he is played and removed that is a tempo loss. Let's imagine some scenarios:

**1. We play Drivnod and pass, he gets removed...** *This is the floor and worst case, we took a turn off and got nothing for our 5 mana investment.*

**2. We play Drivnod and pass, the following turn we play a creature with an ETB trigger Ravenous Chupacabra for example. Chups dies and Drivnod draws us 1 extra card, the following turn Drivnod is removed.** *This is pretty bad too, all Drivnod did was replace himself. Essentially, we paid 5 mana to cycle Drivnod. Now we have to ask ourselves: "If all I do is draw 1 extra card off Drivnod, how many extra cards to I need to draw to make 5 mana worth it? I think the minimum number is 3. Five mana to draw 3 cards... We've just cast a slightly better Ancient Craving*

**3. We play Drivnod and pass, untap and blitz Vindictive Lich. The following turn Drivnod is killed.** *Now we are cooking. We paid 5 mana to kill a creature, make an opponent discards 2 cards, made someone lose 5 life and drew a card off the blitz trigger. In short, Drivnod is at his best when he gets to double a death trigger. For me this is "success."*

Okay, best case scenario is that we get to double a death trigger, and the fastest Drivnod is likely coming down is turn 4. In order to take maximum advantage, we need to have a creature in hand with a "dies" trigger when we untap turn 5, so how many do we need to run?

* Unless your meta is very competitive, and assuming you used the charts to guide your deck building, you will have to mulligan less than everyone else. Afterall, if you used charts, your deck is pretty optimized for its opening hand.

* Don’t be afraid to Mulligan! I can’t tell you how often I see inexperienced/casual players keep a sketchy hand because they don’t want to Mull. **THE FIRST ONE IS FREE! TAKE THE MULL,** and don’t be afraid to go to 6.

#### Can I play Henzie in CEDH

As this deck list has risen in popularity on Moxfield, more and more players have asked me about a CEDH Henzie list. Some are even dumbfounded when I tell them I don’t have and can’t make them one. 
Look, I am an avid CEDH player. I currently play Yuriko, and have played Minsc and Boo, Timeless Heroes, and Vial Smasher the Fierce/Thrasios, Triton Hero. Let me be perfectly straight with you…
Henzie cannot be played at the CEDH level, and still be a Henzie deck. In fact, the deck can’t be played at the CEDH level period… Let’s talk a little about why.

#### Play Pattern
Henzie wants to be played on turn 2. While you could use fast mana like Mox Diamond, Lotus Petal, Jeweled Lotus, + the spirit guides to try and land him turn 1, but you will not have the mana to blitz turn 1 as all these sources of mana are ritual sources (the name comes from Dark Ritual). 

Instead, Henzie wants to be played on turn 2, and to blitz it’s first creature turn 3. In order to present a win this turn we need to blitz Protean Hulk on this turn… That’s asking a lot. Note we have not had time to tutor for Hulk yet. Furthermore, because we want to blitz, we are playing higher cmc spells than most decks. I will show you a couple of sample CEDH lists for Henzie in a little bit, but look as these average cmc of CEDH decks chart:

[![Average-CMC.png](https://i.postimg.cc/k4BfH0v8/Average-CMC.png)](https://postimg.cc/PpnWpFcr)

This is why we lose in CEDH battles. Our opponents stuff is so much cheaper than ours. Thus, it is much easier for them to double spell on a turn than it is for us. Speaking of spells...

#### Interaction

Because Henzie needs other cards to be good, there is less room for interaction in the deck, and we are more likely to be tapped out than our opponents, and the more interaction we run, the few slots we have for blitz creatures. 

While we can cut those blitz creatures, the more blitz creatures we cut, the less we need Henzie… It’s a double edged sword.

There is a clear and substantially better option in Jund. Korvold rewards players for just playing magic. You cracked a treasure? Draw a card. You cracked a fetch land? Draw a card… It costs us 3-4 mana for a blitz and we draw 1 card, and we draw it on end step. I mean look at this nonsense:

You could power up your Henzie deck by including Kiki Combos, DualCaster Mage combos, or Temur Sabertooth/Dockside Extortionist, but you will never be as fast as the fastest decks, and you will never be able to grind card advantage with the midrange decks.

📌 Quick note on Living Death:
The triggers in this sequence were resolved incorrectly. In Commander, when multiple permanents enter the battlefield at the same time (*like after a Living Death*), the triggers go on the stack in turn order, starting with the active player. So, the last player in turn order puts their triggers on the stack last, but they resolve first.

That means Henzie's triggers should have gone on the stack first (they are the active player) and resolved last. Sidisi and Mulldrifter, controlled by players later in turn order, should have resolved before any Henzie triggers. As a result, Terror of the Peaks and Archon of Cruelty triggers from Henzie should have all been declared at once—upfront—before any of them started resolving.

Personally, I would’ve used the Terror triggers to clear the board—or just nuked the Muldrotha player and ended the Spore Frog nonsense outright.
